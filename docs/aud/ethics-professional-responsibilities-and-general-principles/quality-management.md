# Quality Management

Good audits do not happen by accident. They depend on a firm that hires and trains the right people, accepts only engagements it can handle, supervises the work, and checks afterward whether it got things right. The standards call this **quality management**, and they address it at two levels: the firm's system as a whole and each individual engagement. Recent years brought the biggest change in this area in decades. AICPA firms moved from a checklist-style system of quality control to a risk-based system of quality management on December 15, 2025, and the PCAOB's new standard for issuer audit firms, **QC 1000**, takes effect December 15, 2026. This chapter covers the AICPA firm-level standard (SQMS 1), engagement quality reviews, quality management on each engagement (AU-C 220), the PCAOB's QC 1000, and monitoring and remediation.

---

## The Firm's System of Quality Management

**SQMS No. 1**, *A Firm's System of Quality Management*, applies to every firm that performs engagements under the SASs, SSARSs, or SSAEs. Firms had to design and implement their systems by **December 15, 2025**. The system's objective is to provide the firm with **reasonable assurance** that:

- the firm and its personnel fulfill their responsibilities under professional standards and applicable legal and regulatory requirements, and perform engagements in accordance with them; and
- the engagement reports issued by the firm or its engagement partners are appropriate in the circumstances.

Reasonable assurance is high but not absolute; no system can guarantee that every engagement is flawless.

### Eight Components

The system has eight components. Two are **processes** that run through everything else: the firm's risk assessment process and its monitoring and remediation process. The other six cover the areas in which the firm sets quality objectives.

| Component | What it covers |
|---|---|
| **Governance and leadership** | A culture that recognizes quality as essential, with leaders who are responsible and accountable for it |
| **The firm's risk assessment process** | Setting quality objectives, identifying and assessing risks to achieving them, and designing responses |
| **Relevant ethical requirements** | Independence and the other ethical requirements that apply to the firm and its personnel |
| **Acceptance and continuance of client relationships and specific engagements** | Deciding which clients and engagements the firm can take on and keep |
| **Engagement performance** | Direction, supervision, and review; consultation; resolving differences of opinion |
| **Resources** | Human, technological, and intellectual resources, including those obtained from service providers |
| **Information and communication** | Getting quality-related information to the people who need it, inside and outside the firm |
| **The monitoring and remediation process** | Checking whether the system works and fixing what does not |

### A Risk-Based Approach

The old quality control standards told firms which policies to have. SQMS 1 instead asks each firm to manage the risks it actually faces. Through its risk assessment process, the firm establishes **quality objectives** for each component, identifies and assesses **quality risks** (risks with a reasonable possibility of occurring that could, alone or together, adversely affect achieving an objective), and designs and implements **responses** to those risks. A small firm that only performs compilations will end up with a much simpler system than a large firm that audits banks.

Some responses are required of every firm regardless of its risk assessment. These **specified responses** include policies for identifying engagements that need an engagement quality review, for obtaining confirmations of independence, and for dealing with complaints and allegations.

Responsibility is assigned to people. The firm's managing partner (or equivalent) has **ultimate responsibility and accountability** for the system. Other individuals are assigned operational responsibility for the system as a whole, for independence, and for monitoring and remediation. At least annually, the individual with ultimate responsibility evaluates, on behalf of the firm, whether the system is providing reasonable assurance that its objectives are being achieved.

**Example:** Bear Co.'s audit firm identifies a quality risk: teams assigned to clients with complex Level 3 fair value measurements may lack valuation expertise. Its response is a policy requiring each such engagement to include a team member with valuation training and to consult the firm's valuation specialists before concluding on significant estimates.

## Engagement Quality Reviews

An **engagement quality review** is an objective evaluation of the significant judgments made by the engagement team and the conclusions reached, performed by an **engagement quality reviewer** who is not part of the team and completed before the report is dated. The AICPA standard is **SQMS No. 2**; the PCAOB standard is **AS 1220**.

### Which Engagements Need One

Under SQMS 1, a firm's policies must require an engagement quality review when law or regulation requires one, and for any other engagement for which the firm decides a review is an appropriate response to its quality risks. Common triggers include significant public interest (for example, an entity with many outside lenders or investors), unusual circumstances or new industries, significant going concern doubt, and complex estimates. Under AS 1220, every **audit of an issuer**, and every review of an issuer's interim financial information, requires an engagement quality review.

### Who Can Serve

The reviewer must have the competence, capability, time, and authority to perform the review, and must comply with ethical requirements, including objectivity and independence. Because objectivity is hard to maintain when reviewing your own past decisions, both standards impose a cooling-off period: a person who served as engagement partner cannot become the engagement quality reviewer on that engagement for **two years**.

### What the Reviewer Does

The review is an evaluation, not a second audit. The reviewer discusses significant matters and judgments with the engagement partner and team, reviews selected workpapers related to those judgments, considers whether difficult or contentious matters were properly consulted on, evaluates the basis for the team's conclusions on independence, and reads the financial statements and the proposed report. The reviewer is not expected to reperform audit procedures.

Timing is the reviewer's main source of authority. Under SQMS 2, the engagement partner may not **date** the report until notified that the review is complete. Under AS 1220, the firm may not grant the client permission to **use** the report until the reviewer provides concurring approval of issuance.

| Feature | AICPA (SQMS 2) | PCAOB (AS 1220) |
|---|---|---|
| **When required** | When required by law or regulation, or by the firm's policies in response to quality risks | Every issuer audit and interim review |
| **Reviewer** | Not on the engagement team; competent, objective, independent | A partner or equivalent, from the firm or outside it; competent, objective, independent |
| **Cooling-off** | Two years for a former engagement partner | Two years for a former engagement partner |
| **Completion** | Before the report is dated | Concurring approval before the client may use the report |

The SEC's partner rotation rules use the older name **concurring partner** for the engagement quality reviewer on an issuer audit. It is the same role, and it rotates after five years.

**Example:** Grizzly Industries is publicly traded, so its audit requires an engagement quality review under AS 1220 regardless of risk. The reviewer, a partner from another office who has never worked on the engagement, evaluates the team's conclusions on inventory obsolescence and warranty reserves and gives concurring approval before the firm releases its report. Panda Records, a private company, has never had one, but this year it has significant going concern doubt and is seeking financing from several new investors. The firm's policy treats those facts as quality risks that call for an engagement quality review.

## Quality Management on the Engagement

The firm's system creates the environment; the engagement partner applies it to a specific engagement. **SAS 146** rewrote AU-C 220, *Quality Management for an Engagement Conducted in Accordance With GAAS*, for engagements for periods beginning on or after December 15, 2025.

Under AU-C 220, the **engagement partner** has overall responsibility for managing and achieving quality on the engagement and must be sufficiently and appropriately involved throughout. The engagement partner is responsible for:

- **leadership**: setting the expectation that quality comes first on the team;
- **relevant ethical requirements**: making sure the team understands and follows them, including independence;
- **acceptance and continuance**: determining that the firm's policies were followed;
- **engagement resources**: confirming that the team has sufficient and appropriate people, time, and technological resources, such as audit software and analytics or AI tools the firm has approved;
- **engagement performance**: directing, supervising, and reviewing the work, making sure consultation happens on difficult matters, and resolving differences of opinion; and
- **monitoring and remediation**: considering information from the firm's monitoring that affects the engagement.

Before dating the report, the engagement partner takes a step back and determines that the partner's involvement was sufficient to take overall responsibility for the engagement.

SAS 146 also redefined the **engagement team**: all partners and staff performing the engagement and anyone else who performs audit procedures on it, wherever they work, but not the auditor's external specialists or internal auditors providing direct assistance. This definition matters for group audits. Under **SAS 149**, eligible for testing January 1, 2027, component auditors are members of the engagement team and work under the group engagement partner's direction, supervision, and review.

For issuer audits, the PCAOB's **AS 1000** describes the engagement partner's responsibilities, and AS 1201 covers supervision of the engagement team.

## The PCAOB's QC 1000

For firms registered with the PCAOB, **QC 1000**, *A Firm's System of Quality Control*, replaces the PCAOB's interim quality control standards. The PCAOB adopted it in May 2024 and the SEC approved it in September 2024. Its effective date was postponed from December 15, 2025 to **December 15, 2026**.

QC 1000 follows the same risk-based design as SQMS 1 and has the same **eight components**: two processes (the firm's risk assessment process and monitoring and remediation) and six others (governance and leadership; ethics and independence; acceptance and continuation; engagement performance; resources; and information and communication). It also requires the firm to **evaluate its system annually** and report the results to the PCAOB on **Form QC**.

On September 9, 2026, the PCAOB adopted amendments to parts of QC 1000. They still require SEC approval, so they are not settled. Among other things, the amendments would let firms choose the date of their annual evaluation (the 2024 standard sets September 30) and would remove the requirement that firms auditing more than 100 issuers establish an external quality control function.

:::note[When QC 1000 is tested]

The AICPA Board of Examiners has announced that the provisions of QC 1000 not affected by the September 2026 amendments become eligible for testing on **January 1, 2027**. The amendments become eligible in the first calendar quarter beginning six months after the SEC approves them, so no earlier than July 1, 2027. Candidates testing before January 1, 2027 are tested on the PCAOB's interim quality control standards instead.

:::

## Monitoring and Remediation

A system of quality management is only as good as the firm's ability to tell whether it works. Under SQMS 1, the firm designs **monitoring activities**, which combine ongoing monitoring built into the firm's work (such as reviewing how policies are applied) with periodic activities, most importantly **inspections of completed engagements**, selected so that each engagement partner is inspected on a cycle.

When monitoring identifies findings, the firm:

1. evaluates whether the findings indicate a **deficiency** in the system;
2. assesses the **severity and pervasiveness** of each deficiency;
3. investigates its **root cause**; and
4. designs and implements **remedial actions**, then checks whether they worked.

Information from outside the firm feeds the same process. AICPA members' firms that perform accounting and auditing work undergo **peer review**: a system review of the whole quality management system for firms that perform audits or examinations, or an engagement review for firms whose highest level of service is reviews, compilations, or agreed-upon procedures. Firms that audit issuers are also inspected by the **PCAOB**, annually if they audit more than 100 issuers and at least every three years otherwise.

**Example:** During its annual inspection, Ursa Security's audit firm finds that three engagement files did not document the assessed risks of material misstatement at the assertion level. The firm evaluates the finding as a deficiency, because the same gap appeared on several engagements. The root-cause analysis shows that staff were not trained on the new risk assessment templates. The firm requires training, updates its templates, and checks the next round of inspected files to confirm the fix worked.

## Summary

| Topic | Key takeaway |
|---|---|
| **SQMS 1** | Firm-level, risk-based system of quality management, designed and implemented by December 15, 2025 |
| **Eight components** | Governance and leadership; the firm's risk assessment process; relevant ethical requirements; acceptance and continuance; engagement performance; resources; information and communication; monitoring and remediation |
| **Engagement quality review** | Objective evaluation of significant judgments; required for every issuer audit (AS 1220) and as the firm's policies require for nonissuers (SQMS 2); two-year cooling-off for a former engagement partner |
| **AU-C 220 (SAS 146)** | The engagement partner has overall responsibility for managing and achieving quality on the engagement |
| **QC 1000** | PCAOB firm-level standard effective December 15, 2026; tested from January 1, 2027, except the pending 2026 amendments |
| **Monitoring and remediation** | Inspect, evaluate findings, find root causes, remediate, and confirm the fix; peer review and PCAOB inspections add external information |
