# Supplementary Information

Audited financial statements rarely travel alone. A government's statements come with management's discussion and analysis and pension schedules; a private company may add a consolidating schedule or a detailed schedule of expenses; an annual report wraps everything in a letter from the CEO. The auditor's opinion covers the financial statements, so what is the auditor's responsibility for the rest? The answer depends on two questions: is the information **required** by an accounting standard setter, and has the auditor been **engaged to report** on it? This chapter covers required supplementary information, supplementary information on which the auditor reports "in relation to" the financial statements, and, briefly, other information in annual reports, and ends with a comparison of the three.

| Category | AICPA standard | PCAOB standard |
|---|---|---|
| **Required supplementary information** | AU-C 730 | AS 2705 |
| **Supplementary information in relation to the financial statements as a whole** | AU-C 725 | AS 2701 (supplemental information the auditor is engaged or required to report on) |
| **Other information in annual reports** | AU-C 720 | AS 2710 |

---

## Required Supplementary Information

**Required supplementary information (RSI)** is information that a designated accounting standard setter, such as the GASB or the FASB, requires to accompany the basic financial statements. It is not part of the financial statements, but the standard setter considers it essential for placing them in context. Most RSI appears in governmental reports:

- **Management's discussion and analysis** for state and local governments;
- **budgetary comparison schedules** for the general fund and major special revenue funds (when not presented as basic statements); and
- **pension and OPEB schedules**, such as the schedule of changes in the net pension liability and the schedule of contributions.

### Limited Procedures

The auditor does not audit RSI. Instead, the auditor applies **limited procedures**:

1. **Inquire of management** about the methods used to prepare the information, including whether it is measured and presented in accordance with the prescribed guidelines, whether the methods changed from the prior period (and why), and whether any significant assumptions or interpretations underlie it.
2. **Compare the information** for consistency with management's responses, the basic financial statements, and other knowledge obtained during the audit.
3. **Obtain written representations** from management about the RSI, covering the same matters as the inquiries.

These procedures are designed to catch information that is missing, inconsistent, or clearly not prepared according to the guidelines. They do not provide any assurance.

### Reporting on RSI

For nonissuers, the auditor's report always includes an **other-matter paragraph** about RSI. When everything is in order, the paragraph identifies the RSI, states that the framework requires it, states that the auditor applied certain limited procedures, and states that the auditor does not express an opinion or provide any assurance on it. The paragraph is expanded to describe any of these exceptions:

- the RSI is **omitted**;
- some of the RSI departs **materially** from the prescribed guidelines;
- the auditor was **unable to complete** the limited procedures; or
- the auditor has **unresolved doubts** about whether the RSI is presented in accordance with the guidelines.

None of these exceptions affects the opinion on the basic financial statements, because RSI is not part of them. For issuers under AS 2705, the report refers to RSI only when one of these exceptions exists.

**Example:** Bear County presents a schedule of changes in its net pension liability as RSI. The auditor's inquiries reveal that the schedule uses a discount rate different from the one disclosed in the pension note, and management does not correct it. The auditor's opinion on the basic financial statements is unaffected, but the other-matter paragraph states that the schedule departs materially from the GASB's guidelines and describes the departure.

:::caution

An omission of, or departure in, RSI is reported in the other-matter paragraph. It is **never** a reason to qualify the opinion on the financial statements.

:::

## Supplementary Information in Relation to the Financial Statements as a Whole

Other supplementary information is presented with the financial statements but is not required by the framework. Common examples are **consolidating schedules**, **detailed schedules of expenses**, and, in a single audit, the **schedule of expenditures of federal awards**. If the auditor is engaged to report on it, AU-C 725 applies, and the auditor reports on whether the information is **fairly stated, in all material respects, in relation to the financial statements as a whole**.

The auditor can give that opinion only if certain conditions are met. The supplementary information must be derived from, and relate directly to, the same records used to prepare the financial statements, and cover the same period. The auditor must have audited the financial statements and must not have issued an adverse opinion or a disclaimer on them. And the information must accompany the audited financial statements, or the audited statements must be readily available.

### Procedures

The auditor uses the same materiality as in the audit of the financial statements and performs these procedures in addition to the audit:

- inquire of management about the purpose of the information and the criteria used to prepare it;
- determine whether its form and content comply with those criteria;
- understand how it was prepared and whether the methods changed from the prior period;
- **compare and reconcile** it to the underlying records or to the financial statements;
- inquire about any significant assumptions or interpretations;
- evaluate its appropriateness and completeness, considering the results of the audit; and
- obtain written representations from management.

The auditor does not need to perform procedures as extensive as would be required to give an opinion on the information standing alone.

### Reporting

The auditor reports either in an **other-matter paragraph** in the auditor's report on the financial statements or in a **separate report**. When the opinion on the financial statements is modified, the effect carries through:

- If the opinion on the financial statements is **qualified** and the qualified matter affects the supplementary information, the "in relation to" opinion says so.
- If the opinion on the financial statements is **adverse or a disclaimer**, the auditor may not give an "in relation to" opinion. Where law or regulation permits, the auditor may withdraw from the engagement to report on the supplementary information; otherwise, the report states that, because of the significance of the matter, it is inappropriate to express, and the auditor does not express, an opinion on the supplementary information.
- If the auditor concludes that the supplementary information is **materially misstated** and management will not revise it, the auditor modifies the "in relation to" opinion and describes the misstatement, or withholds the report on the supplementary information.

**Example:** Panda Industries, a private company, includes a consolidating schedule that breaks its results out by subsidiary. The auditor reconciles each column to the subsidiaries' trial balances used in the audit and the totals to the consolidated financial statements, inquires about the eliminations, and obtains written representations. The other-matter paragraph states that the schedule is fairly stated, in all material respects, in relation to the consolidated financial statements as a whole.

:::tip

An "in relation to" opinion is narrower than an opinion on the information by itself. It means the information holds up at the level of materiality used for the financial statements as a whole, not that each figure has been audited to a lower threshold.

:::

## Other Information in the Annual Report

When information that is not RSI, and that the auditor is not engaged to report on, appears in the entity's **annual report** alongside the audited statements (for example, a letter to shareholders or operating highlights), it is **other information**. The auditor reads it and considers whether it is materially inconsistent with the financial statements or with the auditor's knowledge from the audit, and remains alert for other information that appears to be materially misstated. The auditor does not express an opinion on it. These responsibilities, and the "Other Information" section of the auditor's report, are covered in [Other Information in Audited Documents](/aud/completing-the-audit-and-reporting/other-information-in-audited-documents).

## Comparing the Three Categories

| Feature | Required supplementary information | Supplementary information (engaged to report) | Other information in the annual report |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Required by the framework?** | Yes | No | No |
| **Auditor's procedures** | Limited procedures: inquire, compare, obtain representations | Procedures in addition to the audit: reconcile to the records and evaluate | Read and consider |
| **Assurance** | None | Opinion "in relation to the financial statements as a whole" | None |
| **Reporting** | Other-matter paragraph (nonissuers) | Other-matter paragraph or separate report | Other information section, as AU-C 720 requires |
| **Effect on the opinion on the financial statements** | None | None | None |

:::tip[Exam Tip]

Ask two questions in order. Is the information **required** by the standard setter? If so, it is RSI: limited procedures, no assurance. If not, was the auditor **engaged to report** on it? If so, apply AU-C 725 and give an "in relation to" opinion. If not, and it appears in the annual report, it is other information to read and consider.

:::
