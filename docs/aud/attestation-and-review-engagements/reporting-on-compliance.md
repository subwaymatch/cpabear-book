# Reporting on Compliance

Lenders, regulators, and grantors often care less about whether the financial statements are fairly stated than about whether the entity followed the rules they set: the covenants in a loan agreement, the terms of a grant, or a regulator's reporting requirements. A CPA can report on compliance in three different settings, and the standard, the work, and the wording of the report all change with the setting. This chapter starts with that map, then works through each setting: a compliance report issued as a by-product of a financial statement audit, a compliance attestation engagement, and a compliance audit of government programs.

---

## Three Settings for Compliance Reporting

| Setting | Standard | What the CPA Reports | Typical Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Report in connection with a financial statement audit** | AU-C 806 | Negative assurance on compliance with contract or regulatory terms that relate to accounting matters | A lender asks the auditor to say whether anything suggests the borrower broke its covenants |
| **Compliance attestation** (compliance is the principal objective) | AT-C 315 | Examination opinion, or agreed-upon procedures findings | A regulator requires an examination of compliance with its rules |
| **Compliance audit** under a governmental audit requirement | AU-C 935, with GAGAS and the Uniform Guidance | Opinion on compliance for each major program | A single audit of a nonprofit that spends federal awards |

The key distinction is whether compliance is **incidental** or **principal**. In an AU-C 806 report, compliance is incidental: the auditor relies on the evidence already gathered in the financial statement audit and plans no separate compliance procedures. In AT-C 315 and AU-C 935 engagements, compliance is a principal objective, and the CPA plans and performs procedures aimed directly at it.

---

## Reports in Connection With an Audit (AU-C 806)

A lender often wants the auditor's comfort that the borrower complied with covenants that are measured from the financial statements, such as a minimum net worth or a maximum debt-to-equity ratio. AU-C 806 allows the auditor to give **negative assurance** on those matters, either in a separate report or in an other-matter paragraph of the auditor's report.

The negative assurance is available only when two conditions hold:

- The auditor has issued an **unmodified or qualified opinion** on the financial statements. It is not available after an adverse opinion or a disclaimer.
- The covenants relate to **accounting matters** that were subjected to audit procedures. The auditor cannot comment on nonaccounting covenants, such as a requirement to maintain insurance.

The report states that nothing came to the auditor's attention that caused the auditor to believe the entity failed to comply with the specified terms insofar as they relate to accounting matters, notes that the audit was not directed primarily toward obtaining knowledge of compliance, describes any noncompliance found, and includes an alert restricting its use to management, those charged with governance, and the other parties to the agreement or the regulator.

**Example:** Bear Co.'s loan agreement requires a minimum net worth of \$5 million. The audited financial statements show net worth of \$6.2 million. After issuing an unmodified opinion, the auditor issues a separate report stating that nothing came to its attention indicating that Bear Co. failed to comply with the net worth covenant. The report's use is restricted to Bear Co. and its lender.

## Compliance Attestation (AT-C 315)

When compliance is the principal objective, the engagement falls under **AT-C 315**. The practitioner may **examine** compliance with specified requirements, or perform **agreed-upon procedures** on compliance or on internal control over compliance. A review is not permitted.

### Examination of Compliance

In an examination, the practitioner obtains **reasonable assurance** about whether the entity complied, in all material respects, with the specified requirements and expresses an **opinion**. The practitioner requests a **written assertion** from the responsible party. The assertion acknowledges management's responsibility for compliance and states whether the entity complied, either as of a point in time or for a period. If the responsible party refuses to provide it, the practitioner ordinarily withdraws from the engagement.

**Example:** Sloth Security holds a government contract that requires specific data handling and security protocols. Management asserts in writing: "Sloth Security complied, in all material respects, with the data security requirements of Contract No. 1234 during the year ended December 31, 20X4." The contracting agency requires an examination, so the CPA tests compliance and issues an opinion.

### Agreed-Upon Procedures on Compliance

In an AUP engagement, the practitioner performs only the procedures the engaging party has agreed to and reports the findings, with no opinion or conclusion. A grantor might, for example, ask the CPA to compare each reimbursement request to supporting invoices and report any differences.

### Modified Opinions on Compliance

Compliance opinions follow the same logic as audit opinions:

| Situation | Opinion |
|---|---|
| The entity complied in all material respects (immaterial exceptions do not matter) | **Unmodified** |
| Material noncompliance that is not pervasive | **Qualified**: "except for the noncompliance described ..., the entity complied, in all material respects ..." |
| Material and pervasive noncompliance | **Adverse**: "the entity did not comply ..." |
| The practitioner cannot obtain sufficient appropriate evidence | **Qualified** or **disclaimer** |

When material noncompliance exists, the practitioner reports directly on compliance (the subject matter), not on management's assertion.

**Example:** Polar Inc. undergoes an examination of its compliance with a regulator's environmental reporting requirements. It failed to file two of six required quarterly reports, and the unreported emissions are a large part of the total. Because the noncompliance is material and pervasive, the CPA issues an **adverse opinion** on compliance.

## Compliance Audits of Government Programs (AU-C 935)

When a governmental audit requirement, such as the Uniform Guidance, calls for an audit of compliance in addition to the financial statement audit, the auditor follows **AU-C 935**, together with GAGAS and the governmental requirement itself. The single audit is the main example. It is required for non-federal entities that expend **\$1,000,000 or more** of federal awards in a fiscal year beginning on or after October 1, 2024 (the threshold was \$750,000 for earlier years).

In a compliance audit, the auditor identifies the compliance requirements that apply to each major program (using the OMB Compliance Supplement), assesses the risk of material noncompliance, tests internal control over compliance, and performs tests of compliance. **Materiality is set for each major program**, not for the financial statements as a whole. The result is a **separate compliance opinion for each major program** and a report on internal control over compliance.

Do not confuse this with the GAGAS report on compliance that accompanies every Yellow Book financial audit. That report covers compliance that could have a direct and material effect on the **financial statements**, and it gives **no opinion**. The single audit report covers each **major program** and **does** give an opinion.

| Report | Scope | Opinion? |
|---|---|---|
| **GAGAS report on internal control and compliance** | Matters affecting the financial statements | No |
| **Single audit report on compliance for each major program** | Requirements that could have a direct and material effect on each major program | Yes, one opinion per major program |

**Example:** Grizzly Foundation, a nonprofit, spends \$3 million of federal awards and has a single audit with two major programs. For a workforce training grant, the auditor issues an unmodified compliance opinion. For a research grant, Grizzly did not meet the cost-sharing (matching) requirement, producing \$180,000 of questioned costs that are material to that program. The auditor issues a **qualified opinion** on compliance for the research grant only. Each major program is evaluated on its own.

## Summary

| Topic | Key Point |
|---|---|
| **Incidental vs. principal** | AU-C 806 relies on the financial statement audit; AT-C 315 and AU-C 935 plan procedures aimed at compliance |
| **AU-C 806** | Negative assurance on accounting-related covenants; only with an unmodified or qualified opinion; restricted use |
| **AT-C 315** | Examination (opinion) or AUP (findings); no review; written assertion requested in an examination |
| **Modified compliance opinions** | Qualified for material, adverse for material and pervasive, disclaimer or qualified for scope limitations |
| **AU-C 935** | Compliance audits under governmental requirements; materiality per major program |
| **GAGAS vs. single audit** | GAGAS compliance report gives no opinion; single audit gives an opinion on each major program |
