# Accounting and Review Services

Many private companies need financial statements but do not need an audit. An owner may want monthly statements for running the business, and a bank may accept reviewed statements for a line of credit. The **Statements on Standards for Accounting and Review Services (SSARSs)**, issued by the AICPA's Accounting and Review Services Committee (ARSC), govern these engagements for **nonissuers**. This chapter climbs the ladder of service one step at a time: preparation (no report, no assurance), compilation (a report, but no assurance), and review (limited assurance). It then covers misstatements found along the way and facts discovered after the report date.

SSARSs never apply to issuers. Reviews of quarterly information are a separate topic, covered in [Review of Interim Financial Information](../completing-the-audit-and-reporting/review-of-interim-financial-information.md): AU-C 930 governs an interim review performed by a nonissuer's auditor, and PCAOB AS 4105 governs interim reviews of issuers.

---

## Overview of Service Levels

| Engagement | Standard | Assurance | Report | Independence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Preparation** | AR-C 70 | None | No report; each page states that no assurance is provided | Not required, and a lack of independence need not be disclosed |
| **Compilation** | AR-C 80 | None | Compilation report | Not required, but a lack of independence must be disclosed in the report |
| **Review** | AR-C 90 | Limited | Review report | Required |

A simple way to keep the three apart: each step up the ladder adds something. A compilation adds a report. A review adds independence, procedures, and limited assurance. All three engagements require a written engagement letter (or other suitable written agreement) signed by the accountant and by management or those charged with governance.

---

## Preparation Engagements (AR-C 70)

In a **preparation engagement**, the accountant is engaged to prepare financial statements from the entity's records. The accountant does not verify the information, does not issue a report, and provides no assurance. Because no report goes out, the standard protects users another way: **each page** of the financial statements must carry a statement such as "No assurance is provided on these financial statements." If the accountant cannot include that statement on each page, the accountant either issues a disclaimer that makes clear no assurance is provided or performs a compilation instead.

Independence is not required, and because there is no report, a lack of independence is never disclosed. The accountant may prepare statements that **omit substantially all disclosures** the framework requires, as long as the omission is clearly indicated on the face of the statements and is not intended to mislead. Any other known departure from the framework must be disclosed in the statements.

**Example:** Bear Co.'s management wants monthly financial statements for internal decision-making. Its CPA prepares a balance sheet and income statement, omits the notes, and puts this legend on each page: "No assurance is provided on these financial statements. Substantially all disclosures required by accounting principles generally accepted in the United States of America are not included."

### Statements Prepared During a Consulting Engagement (SSARS 27)

Accountants who provide client advisory services often produce financial statements along the way. **SSARS 27**, issued in April 2025, clarifies that AR-C 70 does not apply to financial statements prepared as part of a consulting engagement performed under the AICPA's consulting standards (CS 100) when preparing the statements is not the primary objective of that engagement. A CPA who is hired to build a cash flow model and produces monthly statements as a by-product is performing a consulting service, not a preparation engagement. SSARS 27 is effective for periods ending on or after December 15, 2026, and is eligible for testing starting January 1, 2027, so a 2026 candidate would not see this exclusion.

## Compilation Engagements (AR-C 80)

In a **compilation engagement**, the accountant applies accounting and financial reporting expertise to help management present financial statements, and then issues a **compilation report**. The work is still not designed to find errors. The accountant must understand the applicable financial reporting framework and the entity's significant accounting policies, then **read the financial statements** to consider whether they are appropriate in form and free from obvious material misstatement. The accountant does not make inquiries, perform analytical procedures, or verify anything. If the accountant learns that information supplied by management is incorrect or incomplete, the accountant asks for corrected information.

### The Compilation Report

The compilation report is short. It must include:

1. A statement that management is responsible for the financial statements
2. Identification of the financial statements compiled
3. A statement that the accountant performed the compilation in accordance with SSARSs
4. A statement that the accountant did not audit or review the statements, was not required to verify the information provided by management, and does not express an opinion, a conclusion, or any assurance
5. The accountant's or firm's signature, the city and state, and the date (the date the accountant completed the compilation procedures)

A title is **not required**. If management elects to omit substantially all disclosures, the report adds a paragraph saying so.

### Independence in a Compilation

An accountant who is not independent may still perform a compilation but must add a statement to the report: "We are not independent with respect to Polar Inc." The accountant may, but need not, explain why. If the accountant chooses to give reasons, **all** of the reasons must be described.

**Example:** Polar Inc. engages its CPA to compile its annual financial statements. The CPA's spouse owns 10% of Polar Inc., so the CPA is not independent. The CPA can still perform the compilation, with the independence statement in the report, but could not perform a review.

## Review Engagements (AR-C 90)

A **review engagement** gives users **limited assurance**: the accountant reports whether he or she is aware of any material modifications that should be made for the statements to conform with the applicable framework. A review is much more than a compilation and much less than an audit. The accountant does not obtain an understanding of internal control, assess fraud risk, confirm balances, or observe inventory. The evidence comes mainly from **inquiry** and **analytical procedures**, focused on the areas where the accountant believes misstatements are most likely. The accountant also determines materiality for the financial statements as a whole and uses it to design the procedures and evaluate the results.

Three requirements set a review apart from a compilation: the accountant must be **independent**, must perform procedures and accumulate review evidence, and must obtain a **management representation letter**.

### Review Procedures

**Inquiries** are directed to members of management who have responsibility for financial and accounting matters. Typical topics are:

- The accounting principles and practices used and how they were applied
- Procedures for recording, classifying, and summarizing transactions
- Unusual or complex situations, significant journal entries, and related parties
- Subsequent events
- Knowledge of any fraud or suspected fraud

**Analytical procedures** compare recorded amounts with expectations the accountant develops, for example current-year amounts against the prior year or budget, or gross margin trends against the industry. Unexpected differences are investigated, usually through further inquiry. The accountant also obtains evidence that the financial statements agree or reconcile with the accounting records.

### The Review Report

The review report is titled with the word "independent" (for example, "Independent Accountant's Review Report"). It describes management's responsibility and the accountant's responsibility, states that a review is substantially less in scope than an audit and that no opinion is expressed, states that the accountant is required to be independent and to meet other ethical requirements, and gives the conclusion in negative-assurance form:

> "Based on our review, we are not aware of any material modifications that should be made to the accompanying financial statements in order for them to be in accordance with accounting principles generally accepted in the United States of America."

The report is signed, shows the city and state, and is dated no earlier than the date the accountant obtained sufficient review evidence.

:::caution

An accountant who is **not independent cannot perform a review** and must decline or withdraw. A compilation (with disclosure) or a preparation engagement is still possible. For example, if the CPA's brother is Grizzly Inc.'s CFO, the close relative in a key position impairs independence. The CPA must decline the review but could offer a compilation that discloses the lack of independence.

:::

### Compilation and Review Compared

| Feature | Compilation (AR-C 80) | Review (AR-C 90) |
|---|---|---|
| **Assurance** | None | Limited (negative assurance) |
| **Key procedures** | Read the statements for obvious material misstatement | Inquiry and analytical procedures |
| **Independence** | Not required (disclose if lacking) | Required |
| **Representation letter** | Not required | Required |
| **Report title** | Not required | Must include "independent" |

## When the Statements Are Materially Misstated

If the accountant becomes aware during a compilation or review that the statements contain a material departure from the framework (including inadequate disclosure), the first step is to ask management to correct it. If management refuses:

- In a **compilation**, the accountant discloses the departure in a separate paragraph of the compilation report.
- In a **review**, the accountant modifies the conclusion: a **qualified** conclusion, or an **adverse** conclusion if the effects are both material and pervasive, with a paragraph explaining the basis.
- In either engagement, if modifying the report would not adequately communicate the problem, the accountant **withdraws**.

A review has no disclaimer. If the accountant cannot complete the review procedures (for example, because management will not answer key inquiries or refuses to sign the representation letter), the review is incomplete and **no review report is issued**.

---

## Subsequent Discovery of Facts

Sometimes the accountant learns, after the report date, of facts that existed at that date and might have changed the report. This applies to both compilations and reviews, even though the engagement is over. The accountant should:

1. **Discuss** the matter with management (and, if appropriate, those charged with governance)
2. Determine whether the financial statements need **revision**
3. If they do, consider whether management is taking **appropriate action** to inform users who are likely to rely on the statements
4. If management does not act, notify management and those charged with governance that the accountant will seek to prevent further use of the report, and consider whether to obtain legal advice
