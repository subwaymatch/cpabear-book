# Governmental Auditing

Governments and organizations that spend public money answer to taxpayers, legislators, and grantors, not just to creditors and owners. To serve those users, the **Government Accountability Office (GAO)** issues **Government Auditing Standards**, known as the **Yellow Book** or **GAGAS** (generally accepted government auditing standards). GAGAS builds on the AICPA's auditing standards and adds requirements for ethics, competence, quality management, and reporting. This chapter explains when GAGAS applies, the types of GAGAS engagements, the general requirements every GAGAS auditor must meet, and the extra reports a GAGAS financial audit produces. The single audit, which layers federal program requirements on top of GAGAS, is introduced at the end and covered in depth in the next chapter.

---

## What GAGAS Is and When It Applies

The Yellow Book is issued by the **Comptroller General of the United States**, who heads the GAO. It is not an AICPA or PCAOB standard. Auditors follow it when a law, regulation, contract, grant agreement, or policy requires it, which is common for state and local governments, federal agencies, and nonprofits and other entities that receive government awards.

For financial audits, GAGAS **incorporates the AICPA's Statements on Auditing Standards by reference** and adds its own requirements on top. An auditor performing a GAGAS financial audit therefore follows GAAS plus the Yellow Book. GAGAS may also be used together with PCAOB standards.

The current edition is the **2024 revision** of the Yellow Book. It is effective for financial audits, attestation engagements, and reviews of financial statements for periods beginning on or after December 15, 2025, and for performance audits beginning on or after that date.

## Types of GAGAS Engagements

| Engagement | What It Covers | Standards Followed |
|---|---|---|
| **Financial audits** | Opinions on financial statements, plus reports on internal control and compliance | GAAS (AICPA) plus GAGAS |
| **Attestation engagements and reviews of financial statements** | Examinations, reviews, and agreed-upon procedures on subject matter such as internal control, compliance, or performance measures; reviews of financial statements | AICPA attestation or SSARS standards plus GAGAS |
| **Performance audits** | Whether a government program is effective, efficient, and economical, whether it complies with requirements, or how its internal control works | GAGAS only |

Performance audits have no counterpart in the AICPA standards. Their reports describe the audit objectives, scope, and methodology, then present findings and recommendations. The exam concentrates on financial audits, but you should recognize what a performance audit is for.

## General Requirements for GAGAS Auditors

### Ethics and Independence

GAGAS sets out ethical principles (public interest, integrity, objectivity, proper use of government information, and professional behavior) and an **independence conceptual framework**: the auditor identifies threats to independence, evaluates their significance, and applies safeguards to eliminate them or reduce them to an acceptable level. If safeguards cannot do that, the auditor declines or withdraws.

GAGAS is stricter than the AICPA Code in places, particularly for **nonaudit services**. An auditor may never assume management responsibilities for the audited entity. Before providing any nonaudit service, the auditor must determine that management has designated an individual with suitable skill, knowledge, or experience to oversee the service and accept responsibility for its results.

### Competence and Continuing Professional Education

Auditors who plan, direct, perform, or report on GAGAS engagements must complete **80 hours of CPE every two years**. At least **24 of those hours** must relate directly to government auditing, the government environment, or the specific environment in which the audited entity operates.

### Quality Management and Peer Review

The 2024 revision replaced the Yellow Book's system of quality control with a **system of quality management**, modeled on the AICPA's quality management standards. Audit organizations had to design and implement the system by December 15, 2025, and must complete their first evaluation of it by December 15, 2026. Each audit organization must also obtain an **external peer review at least once every three years**.

## Additional Reporting in a GAGAS Financial Audit

The defining feature of a GAGAS financial audit is the extra reporting. Besides the opinion on the financial statements, the auditor reports on **internal control over financial reporting** and on **compliance with provisions of laws, regulations, contracts, and grant agreements**. In practice this is usually one report, issued separately from the opinion and titled along the lines of "Report on Internal Control Over Financial Reporting and on Compliance and Other Matters Based on an Audit of Financial Statements Performed in Accordance With Government Auditing Standards." When the report is separate, the opinion on the financial statements refers to it.

The report:

- Describes the **scope** of the auditor's testing of internal control and compliance
- States whether that testing provided an opinion on internal control or compliance (it does not; the tests were performed as part of the financial statement audit)
- Reports **significant deficiencies** and **material weaknesses** in internal control
- Reports instances of **fraud** and **noncompliance** that have a material effect on the financial statements
- Includes the views of responsible officials on the findings

Two points are tested often. First, the report is issued **even when nothing is found**; the auditor simply states that no material weaknesses or reportable noncompliance were identified. Second, it gives **no opinion** on internal control or on compliance. Noncompliance and fraud that are less than material but still warrant the attention of those charged with governance are communicated in writing to officials of the audited entity.

Each finding is developed using the **elements of a finding**: the **criteria** (what should be), the **condition** (what is), the **cause** (why it happened), and the **effect** (the consequence), usually followed by a recommendation.

**Example:** Polar Foundation, a nonprofit that receives state and federal grants, has a GAGAS audit. The auditor issues an unmodified opinion on the financial statements. The separate report on internal control and compliance describes the scope of testing, reports one significant deficiency in how grant expenditures are tracked, and states that the tests disclosed no instances of noncompliance required to be reported.

## Single Audits in Brief

The **Single Audit Act** (as amended) and OMB's **Uniform Guidance** (2 CFR Part 200, Subpart F) require a **single audit** of any non-federal entity that **expends \$1,000,000 or more** in federal awards during its fiscal year. The \$1,000,000 threshold applies to fiscal years beginning on or after October 1, 2024; the threshold for earlier years was \$750,000.

A single audit is a GAGAS financial audit plus a **compliance audit** of the entity's major federal programs. On top of the GAGAS reports described above, the auditor issues an **opinion on compliance for each major program**, a report on **internal control over compliance**, an opinion on the **schedule of expenditures of federal awards** in relation to the financial statements, and a **schedule of findings and questioned costs**. The [Uniform Guidance for Single Audits](./uniform-guidance-for-single-audits.md) chapter covers the threshold, major program determination, compliance testing, and the reporting package.

## Summary

| Topic | Key Point |
|---|---|
| **Yellow Book (GAGAS)** | Issued by the Comptroller General through the GAO; builds on GAAS for financial audits |
| **Engagement types** | Financial audits; attestation engagements and reviews of financial statements; performance audits |
| **Independence** | Conceptual framework; stricter limits on nonaudit services; never assume management responsibilities |
| **CPE** | 80 hours every two years, including 24 hours directly related to government |
| **Quality** | System of quality management (2024 revision); external peer review at least every three years |
| **Extra GAGAS reports** | Internal control over financial reporting and compliance; issued even with no findings; no opinion |
| **Single audit** | Required at \$1,000,000 or more of federal awards expended (fiscal years beginning on or after October 1, 2024) |
